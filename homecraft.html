Homecraft

Master thesis | 2024 - 2025

Making shelter design and construction accessible to non-experts through CNC-aided design and Makerspaces.


Learn more

What was the project about?


The focus of my thesis stems from an earlier project I did together with Amir Ghadiri during CORE (Computation for Earthquake Resilience and Recovery), where we concentrated on improving shelter design in response to the challenges posed by disasters like earthquakes.

Natural and man-made disasters displace millions of people worldwide every year, creating an urgent need for shelters that are not only safe and habitable but also adaptable to the diverse cultural and environmental needs of displaced communities.

Recognizing the broader challenges faced by displaced communities, such as the need for rapid deployment, cultural adaptability, sustainability, and community empowerment, the main objective of this thesis was to see if with the help of computational design and digital fabrication it is possible to let individuals with limited expertise in architecture, engineering, and design, to design and actualize personalized shelter solutions within their own community using the resources that are locally available without the need for expert involvement.

To explore how technologies like CNC machines, combined with accessible materials such as plywood, could democratize shelter construction, making it feasible for non-experts to create sustainable, functional housing solutions rooted in their own culture and environment. By empowering individuals to take an active role in the creation of their homes, which supports community recovery, autonomy, and resilience. Ultimately, hoping to contribute to a larger conversation around self-reliance, accessibility, and sustainability in the built environment, emphasizing the transformative potential of community-driven spaces and open-source design.

The concept

The proposal of this project was to do this through the creation of an online environment where individuals can design and manufacture their own customizable shelters, tailored to local materials, cultural traditions, and specific needs. By guiding users through the design process with computational tools, the system ensures the structural integrity and resilience of each shelter, while still allowing for personal expression and adaptability. Alongside the design, users are given the tools to produce each custom element locally through digital fabrication technologies like CNC-routing, promoting a strong sense of ownership, empowerment, and self-reliance within their communities.


Thus the concept of the project consists of 2 parts, the physical concept (the geometry, structure, layout, materiality, climate design etc.) as well as the digital design (parametric model, user interface, database etc.)

For the physical concept a few main key points were taken into consideration, among which modularity and expandability, using wooden plate materials for the structure so they could be produced with CNC, customizable layouts, use of local materials, sustainability, longevity, ease of assembly as well as deconstruction, natural ventilation and daylight, thermal performance and cultural aesthetic flexibility. Which resulted in the following design:

A modular, CNC-machined wooden frame system designed to be affordable, customizable, and easy to assemble or expand by non-experts using locally sourced materials. Inspired by a simple, universally recognizable home shape, a rectangular layout with a gabled roof, the structure balances efficiency, adaptability, and comfort. Users can customize layouts, adjust dimensions, and choose materials based on local availability and personal needs. The system supports phased growth, allowing extensions horizontally or vertically as circumstances change.









Climate-responsive design principles like passive ventilation, thermal insulation, and raised foundations ensure adaptability across different environments.



As well as using frictionfit wood on wood joinery to make assembly as wel ass disassembly easy.
